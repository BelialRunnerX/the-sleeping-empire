# The Living Frontier: visual implementation

Aetherbound 3.7 uses a perspective camera and modeled 3D characters, enemies, scenery, resource objects, buildings and workstations in every playable region. Hero portraits and the title retain original illustrated assets. The portrait references informed the sculpted jaw, tapered swept locks, raised collars, trims and segmented armor. Beveled hardware, irregular foliage and rock meshes, and terrain bump shading extend the material detail. The older sprite and terrain atlases remain in the repository as release art; see [asset provenance](assets/ASSETS.md).

The game uses cel-shaded meshes and a small set of shared materials, articulated arms and legs, capes, equipment geometry, directional lighting, optional shadows, foliage, fog, sky gradients and animated water. Orbit has a dark sky, planetary silhouette, alloy hull sections, panels, a coolant trench and lower jump gravity. The science-fiction visuals are code-authored; the game does not ship Minecraft assets or imported skeletal models.

The camera smooths heading, pitch, zoom and follow position. Terrain clearance, solid footprints and ray tests against nearby scenery retract the camera as needed. Wheel, orbit drag, Left Ctrl orbit toggle, pan drag, reset and buttons are available on desktop; touch supports tap, orbit drag, pinch and twist. Graphics quality, reduced motion and camera orbit preferences save per browser. F8 hides the HUD.

Resource meshes are wired to gathering and depletion. Caches animate their lids, rubble disappears once cleared, and relay lights mark synchronization. Three modeled production stations retain progress rings, status labels and sparks. Dawnwake and the Warden encounter retain their fixed anchors while terrain height and outlying landmarks vary by seed and realm. Gear now changes the 3D silhouette. Longswords, daggers, spears, bows, projectors, light and heavy armor, and layered shields have distinct geometry. Geode crystal size reflects its growth stage. The orbital planet uses a surface shader with lit bands. The active mesh window is 49 chunks. Heights, colors and normals agree at shared edges, and unloaded chunk meshes are released.

`npm run test:visual` renders all five 3D regions at multiple graphics settings, checks perspective mesh ray picking, cycles all twelve appearances, and checks shaders and geometry lifetime. Structural limits are 1,200 draw calls, 600,000 triangles and fewer than 16 textures in the tested views. These checks are independent of a frame-rate claim. See [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) for authored anime rigs, animations and biome art priorities.
