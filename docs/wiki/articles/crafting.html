Crafting and industry

Native development snapshot · Reviewed 18 November 2026

Field assembly bootstraps the native factory chain. Processing then depends on machines, fuel, power and reserved inputs.

Version matters. This is an implementation reference, not a promise that every feature is in your installed build. Provisional adaptations and limitations are stated explicitly. Controls describe the documented native snapshot and may differ in the Windows GUI handoff.

Hand-assembled starting machines

The provisional native costs are: Furnace, 8 stone + 2 logs; Alloy Crucible, 8 stone + 4 iron ingots; Fabricator, 4 iron + 2 copper + 2 steel ingots. These are closure adaptations, not claimed canonical Sixth Edition recipes.

Factory progression

Coal fuels the furnace; current starter tools can harvest coal, copper and iron. Furnace processing provides ingots and carbon. The crucible needs power to produce steel, and the fabricator needs power to manufacture more machines and components.

Exact inputs and capacity

Assembly totals exact integer micro-volume units across slots. It privately consumes inputs before checking final output capacity, so a freed slot may receive the kit. Missing materials or insufficient space leave the live pack unchanged. Unique gear is never a recipe input.

Queues and recovery

Machine production uses paid/reserved inputs and active progress, not a free preview output. Kits place existing machine owners. Recovery requires the machine’s applicable empty/idle/fuel/energy conditions and inventory capacity; it must not duplicate outputs or manufacture free fuel.
