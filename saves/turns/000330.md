## Played record

**The Blue Satchel**
*League Senate observation gallery · Same sitting*

At your request, the chair asks the clerk to find out why the rescue tug wants the berth number. You stay at the brass rail. An attendant sets a cup of tea beside you, and the steam smells faintly of orange peel.

The tug answers on the chamber speaker. Behind its pilot’s voice, you can hear the steady ticking of a relay.

“Medical handoff. One of those passengers left a blue satchel in our cabin. Her evening dose is in it. We can leave it with whoever takes custody.”

“Whoever receives the satchel,” the chair corrects. “Nobody here is taking custody of the passengers. Can you leave it with the harbourmaster without knowing where they dock?”

A pause. Then: “Yes. Send me a receipt.”

The clerk switches back to the passenger cabin. The woman who spoke earlier leans close enough for you to see the fine salt dried white along the seam of her coat.

“Blue canvas, broken buckle?” she asks. “That’s mine. I thought I’d lost it.” She looks offscreen for a moment. “Please take the bag. Please don’t tell them where I am.”

The clerk draws one clean line through **BERTH NUMBER?** and writes **MEDICAL DELIVERY: HARBOURMASTER** beneath it. The tug’s willingness to make the handoff answers the immediate question. It doesn’t settle what happened last time.

Then the harbourmaster’s lamp lights green. “Passenger gate is open,” she says. “Ship’s still coming in. We’ll confirm once everyone’s ashore.”
