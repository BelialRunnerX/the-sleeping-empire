**A Name at the Harbour**
*Greyhaven harbour · Shortly after docking*

The wind off the water is cold enough to make your ears sting. Down on the quay, the passenger ship creaks against its ropes. The tug is berthed out of sight around the breakwater.

You introduce yourself as Crow. The woman with the blue satchel is sitting on a bench under the harbour office awning. She shifts her bag to make room beside her.

“Nessa,” she says. “You can use that name. Just don’t put it over the harbour speaker.”

She unbuckles the satchel and takes out the medicine bottle. A scrap of paper comes with it, folded twice around the glass. She reads it, then holds it out to you. The pencil has smudged where the paper rubbed against the bottle.

*Last time, you asked me not to let them take you. I heard you. I’m ready to say that where someone will write it down.*

“That’s the deckhand’s writing,” Nessa says. “She used to leave notes by my bunk when she took the early watch.” Her thumb stays on the folded edge. “If she’s ready to talk, I’d like to hear what she says. But I don’t want to be in the room with the pilot.”

Through the open harbour office door, you hear the harbourmaster on the receiver. “Tell her to hold for a moment. I’m finding somewhere she can speak privately.” So far, nobody has given the tug’s crew permission to come ashore.
