### CCXXXIX — The Cargo That Could Speak
Elys and Aletheia enter black ceramic transport corridors and discover a chamber with twenty-three recesses, most empty, and **two living Light-Born in separate protected transport capsules**, verifiably among the original 23 historic Cradle transfers. One is conscious, speaks fearfully about being moved/forced to forget, and has no presently recovered name. The other is alive but medically unstable and consciousness unverified. Environmental life support cannot simply be removed; neither is fully developmentally independent. The system attempts coercive memory regression; Elys's **unlimited innate negation** stops the coercion without disabling life support. Elys reassures the first that he is not a keeper/collector; Aletheia is gentle with captives yet eager to punish actual offenders. The living subjects have been used as **phase-navigation calibration references** in a larger installation, not simply energy fuel. The unknown Auditor identifies Elys Somion as Imperial sovereign and orders destruction of the platform. **Two of 23 transfer survivors confirmed alive; other 21 fates still unresolved**. The archival count of 17 demonstrated independent agency at time of transfer remains unchanged and does not imply maturity.

### CCXL — The Auditor's Mistake
The Auditor tries remotely to shut down life support, collapse chambers and erase records. Elys negates these hostile effects completely while preserving essential systems. Aletheia destroys hostile automated interior security devices and halts a secret outbound data transmission while preserving local buffers. They recover a provisionally authenticated internal engineering assessment showing living resonance subjects were used for phase-navigation drift correction in a very large mobile Asterion installation. It shows **engineers had proposed independently stabilized mechanical alternatives** to compelled living references, but later administrators rejected these on cost/schedule/strategy grounds. This undermines any unsupported assertion that the coercion was absolutely necessary; no alternative has yet been built or verified.
The Auditor alleges a coming existential threat and that the Night Engine prevents systems' exposure to “something that was here before the stars.” **This is the antagonist's unverified assertion, NOT established narrator fact.** The Auditor's identity, agenda and nature of the alleged darkness remain UNKNOWN.
Elys creates an independent **medical transport cradle** around existing capsules, without dismantling their environmental fields; this is rescue support, not personal inventory, gear, property or a minted artifact. The conscious captive says it wishes to remember its name; no name is restored yet. Matriarch Seven acknowledges a secure request to prepare safe, independently reviewed receiving care at the Cradle; neither captive has arrived there by the end of the round. No owner is assigned; independent advocates and consent still apply. The transfer installation no longer answers Auditor's destructive commands.
A recovered phase-correction **schedule** narrows the next Asterion moving rendezvous corridor beyond the original seven broad windows; it remains a forecast subject to enemy changes, not exact verified location. Distant telemetry shows a vast **unidentified dark object** haloed by manipulated starlight matching Asterion routing patterns, but its identity is not confirmed. Elys and Aletheia stand ready to pursue after stabilizing rescue logistics. Last spoken beat: Aletheia sees the large object, delights at the prospect of a worthy hostile, and declares she intends to be “positively insufferable.” The next round begins with medical extraction logistics and investigation of the newly narrowed corridor, not an already-completed Night Engine encounter.
