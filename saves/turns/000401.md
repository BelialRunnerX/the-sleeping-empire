# Turn 000401 — Under Their Own Name

**Arc:** The Genosis Arc.  
**Location:** Inside the League Senate chamber, Greyhaven, Genosis.  
**Player action:** Crow observes; he issues no new direction.  
**Record:** Exact played voice response.

## Played scene

When the bell calls the senators back, Ordel’s seat is empty. The clerk has reached the second paragraph of the amended motion by the time its senator returns, smelling of rain.

“Did you find a courier?” Tessa asks as he sits.

“Found two. Neither would take a Senate packet without asking whether the doors would still be open when he came back.”

The clerk keeps reading. Governments may exchange the names of officials who can arrange assistance under powers they already have. Joining creates no obligation to send supplies, spend money, or surrender authority. Any later commitment comes back to the governments making it.

Tessan’s senator asks for one change before the vote. “Put the office beside the name. If your road commissioner dies or leaves office, my people need to know where to take the letter.”

That goes in without objection. The motion carries, twenty-six to six, with three abstentions. One of the senators who votes no gives the clerk the name of his country’s road commissioner before sitting down. “My instructions were against a League office,” he says. “I have permission to give you that.”

The clerk has barely closed the voting book when Nareth’s delegate asks what will happen to Sera Dain’s account.

“It’s entered,” the chair says.

“Can someone outside this building read it?”

The chair looks at the clerk. The register can be consulted by any delegation, he says. Sending copies to the member governments is a separate decision.

“Then I move that we send them,” says Nareth’s delegate. “Along with any firsthand account another witness wishes to submit under their own name.”

Ordel’s senator puts a hand on the back of the bench in front of him. Nareth’s delegate turns towards him, expecting him to second it. He takes his hand away.

“I’d like to read mine before I put my name to it,” he says. “I gave the first account to my brother. He asked how many were hurt. I told him everybody was alive. That was true by the time I told him.”

The chair gives the clerk until tomorrow to collect statements and circulate the motion. The courier from Ordel is still in the corridor. He takes the contact list in a sealed packet. Across the hall, Nareth’s delegate sits down with Sera’s account and starts making the copy she’ll send home herself.
