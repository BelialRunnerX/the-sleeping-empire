# Turn 000362 — The Dredging Dues

**Arc:** The Genosis Arc.  
**When:** Two days after the first Tessan fishing renewal receipt; no established calendar date.  
**Player action:** None. Crow remains on his quiet flagship hiatus; the Senate and its guests act.  
**Record:** Exact played voice narration.

## Played scene

Two days after the first fishing receipt reaches Tessan, the Nareth and Ordel delegations bring their river agreement to the Senate. They bring one copy each. The maps at the back are identical. The handwritten notes in the margins aren’t.

Aurelia’s registrar looks up from the Ordel copy. “Why does your copy say the grain tariff is zero?”

“Because there’s no tariff on the grain,” says Ordel’s senator.

“Then what are these payments?”

“Dredging dues,” says Sera Dain of Nareth. “Ordel keeps the lower channel open. Our barges pay by the load.”

The registrar turns to Aurelia. Under the Imperial decree, he explains, the river now runs entirely within one Imperial region. The treaty office can carry over the agreement, but the treasury sees a charge on internal trade. It wants the charge suspended while it decides whether Ordel can collect it.

“If you suspend it on Monday,” says Ordel’s senator, “my assembly will lay off the dredge crews on Friday. I’ve got six weeks before the grain starts coming down.”

“And if we call it a harbour fee,” Sera says, “Ordel can raise it without asking us. We’ve had that argument before. In our copy it’s on page nine.”

The clerk turns to page nine. Someone has drawn a little fist beside the relevant clause.

Aurelia studies the two books. “How much does it cost to keep the channel open for one season?”

Ordel has the figures. Nareth has a different set. The gap between them is the cost of replacing a dredger that broke its winch last winter. Ordel has been charging half the cost to the barges. Nareth says it agreed to pay for the channel, never for a new ship.

“Send both sets of figures,” Aurelia says. “The barges can keep running at the current rate while we review the accounts. Put the disputed portion in a separate account. If I order it returned, I want the money there to return.”

Ordel’s senator starts to object. Sera gets in first. “And if we settle it ourselves before you do?”

Aurelia’s registrar reaches for his microphone. Aurelia answers before he can.

“Send me what you settle.”

Outside the chamber, Sera asks Ordel’s senator to stay for lunch. He says he has a train. She tells him she knows. Her delegation has two seats on the same one. By the time the clerk comes out with their books, they’re already arguing about how many more winters the old dredger has in it.
