**Two Addresses**
*League Senate chamber · Morning*

At your request, the envoy looks up both authorities before the notice goes any further. Two blue lines appear on the quartz display.

“The tug is registered with the **Meridian Civil Registry**. Its maritime office can review the pilot’s conduct and the company’s use of a rescue vessel. The station is licensed by the **Vey Transit Authority**. They’re the ones who can look at whether people were prevented from leaving.”

“And do we hear back?” the chair asks, following your second question.

“The registry acknowledges delivery. If either authority opens or declines a review, the League can ask to be notified. That won’t give you access to the case file, and silence won’t mean they cleared the company.”

You ask the chair to put the proposed notice to a vote. He reads the motion aloud: refer the concern to the two named authorities, request acknowledgment and notice of their decisions, and keep witness names, statements, and recordings in Greyhaven. The motion does not authorize a League investigation or deployment.

A green light comes on at the first desk, then another across the aisle. The red lights are fewer. The clerk waits for the last senator to register an abstention, then reads the result.

“Motion carried.”

The chair signs the authorization and hands it to the clerk. “Have the harbourmaster check the facts before I sign the final notice. If a witness says something happened, write *a witness says*. We’re voting to pass on a concern, not to turn it into a finding.”

The clerk places the authorization and the still-blank notice in the same folder. The vote is on the record. The notice hasn’t left Greyhaven.
