## Played record

**What the Pilot Was Told**
*Greyhaven harbour · Tug berth · Same evening*

You leave Sera in the interview room with the harbourmaster and return to the tug. The pilot is still aboard. You ask him plainly what the situation is, without telling him what Sera has said.

“The company owns this tug,” he says. “Last time, they sent us to a disabled transport. We brought its people aboard, and dispatch told me to take them to a company station. I took ‘crew transfer’ to mean they worked for us. At the station, security led them off. I didn’t ask whether they wanted to go.”

A loose chain taps against the hull. The pilot looks down at it before going on.

“Tonight was different. Their ship wasn’t disabled. Dispatch told me to follow it and report where it landed. I asked for the berth because of the medicine, yes. I was also expected to pass that number back to the company.”

“Expected” is his word. When he opens the dispatch on his wrist slate, the line he shows you is more direct:

**REPORT LANDING SITE AND BERTH. DO NOT MAKE CONTACT WITH PASSENGERS.**

“I’ve reported Greyhaven,” he says. “I haven’t sent a berth number. They’ve asked twice since we tied up.”

He holds the slate where you can read it from the quay. The message shows the company dispatch seal, but the signature field names an office, not a person.
