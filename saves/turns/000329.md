# Two Columns

**League Senate, observation gallery · Same sitting**

The clerk sets a little receiver on the rail below you. Rain ticks against the high windows. When he turns the brass dial, the whole chamber hears a burst of static, then the hollow clang of a hatch.

“This is the passenger cabin,” a woman says through the speaker. “The captain said you wanted to hear from one of us.”

“We did,” says the senator by the aisle. “What would you like the harbourmaster to know?”

“That we want to get off. Last time we said that, the tug’s crew told us they couldn’t release us to anyone but the company that sent them. If you berth us alongside them, we’re still asking the same people for permission.”

A second voice, farther from the receiver: “Ask if we can bring our bags.”

The chair leans toward the microphone. “You may bring your bags. The harbourmaster can admit you on his own authority. He’ll keep your berth separate from the tug’s. You don’t have to make your case over an open channel before you’re allowed ashore.”

A few benches down, a senator who hasn’t spoken all morning lowers his glasses. “Has the tug asked us to hand anyone over?”

The clerk checks the incoming slips. “Not yet. It’s asked us to tell it which berth they’ve been assigned.”

From the gallery you can see the two columns on his page. Under **BERTH**, he’s written *accepted*. Under **ESCORT**, there’s still only *not requested*. He starts a third column and looks up at the chair.

“Do we normally give out berth numbers?”
