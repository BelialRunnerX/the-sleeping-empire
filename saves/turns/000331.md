## Played record

**Two Berths, One Landing**
*League Senate observation gallery · Same sitting*

From the gallery, you propose berthing both ships, but giving only the passenger vessel permission to disembark. A small military detail can collect the satchel from the tug and deliver it to its owner. The tug’s crew stays aboard for now.

The chair glances up at you, then turns to the clerk. “Record those separately. Permission to dock is not permission to come ashore.”

He approves a small port military detail for the handoff and nothing more. The harbourmaster assigns the vessels separate berths and gives each crew only its own assignment. On the chamber receiver, you hear the tug pilot read back the order: “Tie up. Remain aboard. Pass the satchel over. Understood.”

A few minutes later, the passenger vessel’s gangway drops with a clang. A man comes down first, gripping a battered suitcase with both hands. The woman in the salt-stained coat follows, squinting in the harbour lights. Nobody asks either of them to explain themselves before they step ashore.

When the detail arrives, one of the soldiers holds out a blue canvas bag with a split brass buckle. The woman checks the name on the medicine bottle inside, then hugs the satchel against her ribs.

“Thank you,” she says. “I wasn’t sure you could take the bag without taking us back.”

The harbourmaster confirms both ships are berthed and all passengers are ashore. The tug’s crew is still aboard. Then a new request comes over the speaker.

“Harbourmaster,” says the tug pilot, “one of my deckhands wants to speak to you privately. It’s about the last time we picked these people up. Does she have to come ashore to do that?”
