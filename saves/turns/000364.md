# Turn 000364 — Both Banks

**Arc:** The Genosis Arc.  
**When:** After the ferry-landing draft was posted; no calendar date established.  
**Player action:** None. Crow remains in his quiet flagship hiatus.  
**Record:** Exact played voice narration.

## Played scene

By noon the draft is nailed to the ferry shelter on both banks. By three, someone on the Ordel side has written a question beneath the proposed joint schedule.

Who gets to tell the dredger to stop?

Sera Dain reads it while she waits for the crossing. The ferry is taking passengers on foot. The grain carts have been turned back, and a driver is standing beside his horse arguing that an empty cart ought to count as a pedestrian.

On the other bank, Ordel’s senator has borrowed the weigh house for the public meeting. He has the old dredger’s forewoman beside him. She came straight from the yard and has set a little iron tooth on the table to keep the draft from blowing away.

“That came off the bucket this morning,” she tells Sera. “I can weld it back on. I can probably weld it back on again next month. How many times would your assembly like me to put in the estimates?”

Sera opens her notebook. “How many weeks can you give us?”

“Six, if I move her down to the ferry cut this week. Less if I have to drag her up to the grain landings first. You can buy her twin tomorrow and have exactly the same problem. There’s one stretch of river where both boats would need to work at the same time, and there’s only room for one.”

A woman in the front row raises her hand. She runs the smallest of Nareth’s grain depots. When the channel closes, she says, the big merchants put their loads on the railway. She can’t fill enough wagons to get their rate. Last year she paid three men to shovel grain out of a wet hold because she couldn’t afford to turn back.

Across the aisle, the ferryman asks whether she remembers what happened the week they took the dredger upriver.

“I remember you stopped taking carts.”

“I stopped taking the midwife across after dark.”

The clerk waits for Sera or the senator to call on the next speaker. Neither does.

The forewoman pulls the river map towards her. She asks the ferryman to mark the shallowest place on his crossing. Then she asks the depot keeper where her boat started taking water. The two marks are nearly a mile apart by road and less than two hundred yards apart by river. The forewoman studies them for a while. She asks whether the grain boats need the whole channel, or just enough room to pass one at a time.

“One at a time would do,” the depot keeper says. “If the big carriers have to queue with the rest of us.”

That gets a louder answer from the room than anything anyone has said about the Empire.

By dusk the two delegations have agreed to pay for a fresh sounding before they divide up the season. The crews will check both stretches from the same boat and publish the depths on both banks. Ordel’s senator will ask his assembly to keep the forewoman’s crew on for the survey. Sera will ask Nareth to share the cost. The replacement dredger is still on the table. It’s the only thing the forewoman has put a circle around.

At Greyhaven, the clerk sends Aurelia the minutes. Her registrar replies that the treasury will keep the disputed dues in the separate account while the survey goes ahead. He also sends the form for registering a joint river authority. It’s the first form any of them has seen with room for two governments to sign. The heading calls them both municipal administrations.

Sera is back at the ferry shelter when the form reaches her. The ferryman lends her his pencil. She crosses out the heading, writes Nareth and Ordel above it, and sends the form back unsigned.

Before the last ferry leaves, the forewoman takes down the question from the shelter wall and folds it into the pocket where she keeps her work orders. She’s decided to bring it to the survey.
