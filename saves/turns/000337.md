## Played record

**Room at the Quay**
*Greyhaven harbour · Tug berth · Same evening*

You tell the pilot he’s welcome to keep the tug berthed. The harbourmaster will take statements from everyone, separately. Until it’s clear what his company is doing, you won’t grant his crew entry visas. In the meantime, Greyhaven will see to their needs right here.

“So we’re not being turned away?” he asks.

“No,” the harbourmaster says. “You have a berth. You don’t have permission to go into the city. If you need something, ask me, not dispatch.”

The pilot looks back through the wheelhouse glass. Two crew members are watching from inside. “Hot food, if you have it. We were meant to be back by supper.” He takes a breath. “And I’ll give that statement first.”

Soon the smell of onion stew drifts across the quay. A port steward carries covered bowls up the gangway. In the interview room, the harbourmaster tells Sera she can stay in a room at the port overnight if she wants. She doesn’t have to go back aboard to be fed or have a bed. It isn’t an entry visa, and it isn’t conditional on giving a statement.

Sera takes her coat off for the first time since she came ashore. “Then I’ll stay. And yes, I still want to talk.”

The harbourmaster opens two files: one for the crew, one for the passengers. No one has to tell their story in front of anyone from the other ship. As she sets the recorder between herself and Sera, a clerk brings in a fresh message from the company.

“They’re asking whether their own representative can come to Greyhaven to collect the passengers’ statements.” He puts the message down. “They’re calling it a welfare visit.”
