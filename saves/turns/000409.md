# Turn 000409 — Instructions From Home

**Arc:** The Genosis Arc.  
**Location:** Inside the League Senate chamber, Greyhaven, Genosis.  
**Player action:** Crow observes; he issues no new direction.  
**Record:** Exact played voice response.

## Played scene

The clerk starts to gather the papers. Tessa puts her hand on the draft.

“I’m not finished. The chair asked for a physician. One was refused. If that happens again, what will your officer do when someone asks him to open the door?”

“If he closed it on my authority, he’ll open it.”

“And if the Emperor closed it?”

Aurelia takes a moment before answering. “I could ask him to open it.”

Tessa lets go of the paper. “So could I. I did.”

Ordel’s senator rises. His chair catches on the flagstones and tips behind him. He leaves it there. “I had my hands on Sera when he told me to sit. I sat. I signed that because it happened. Now my brother has your statement and wants to know what I made up.”

“I have made no finding about your testimony,” Aurelia says.

“Then why did you put your seal on that word?”

The clerk looks up. Aurelia rests her hands on the witness table. “The statement gives the Empire’s position on the packet. I have given you the full extent of that position.”

Nareth’s delegate opens Sera’s account. “My assembly heard her. They sent me here with instructions to keep our seat, because the river agreement and the winter grain contracts still have to be settled. They also told me to come home if this hearing produced no protection beyond the Regent’s own orders.” She looks at Aurelia. “I was hoping I’d misunderstood my instructions.”

Across the table, Tessan’s senator closes her folder. “My instructions tell me to stay. Forty-seven crews are waiting on those renewals. I’d like the record to show I can think it’s dangerous to stay and still have a reason to do it.”

The chair lets the clerk finish writing. Then she turns to Aurelia. “Issue your instruction. We’ll send your exact words home with it. I intend to ask each government whether its delegate is authorised to attend the next sitting.”

The clerk reaches for the calendar. There are three votes due that morning. The chair asks him to leave them on the page until they know who’s coming.
