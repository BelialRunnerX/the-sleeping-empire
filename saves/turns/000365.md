# Turn 000365 — Same Bottom

**Arc:** The Genosis Arc.  
**When:** Three mornings after the ferry landing meeting, with the reply the following morning; no calendar date established.  
**Player action:** None; Crow remains quietly aboard.  
**Record:** Exact played voice narration.

## Played scene

Three mornings later, the forewoman brings the survey boat in under the ferry cable. Both assemblies have voted her enough money for three days of soundings. Ordel’s treasurer has made a point of counting the third day as a Sunday, when the crews cost more. Nareth has paid its half anyway.

Sera watches from the landing as the forewoman’s apprentice takes the first reading. He calls it out to two clerks, one from each bank. The clerks write it down. At the next mark he calls a number so low that both of them ask him to take it again.

“Same bottom,” he says, bringing up the sounding line. “I can ask it louder.”

By noon they’ve found enough water for the ferry and the smaller grain boats. The bigger boats would have to sail half loaded until the cut is cleared. Sera had expected the grain merchants to object to the delay. Instead their spokesman asks whether his company can pay the dredger’s wages outright and have the cut ready for its first convoy.

The forewoman wipes her hands on a rag. “You can pay the wages. Who do you think I’ll be working for when the ferryman asks me to stop?”

The spokesman looks across the landing. The ferryman is standing beside a woman with a doctor’s bag. He knows her. Everyone at the landing knows her.

“Put the ferry hours in the agreement,” he says at last. “And put my convoy in it too. I have twenty people waiting to know when they’re working.”

That evening, Nareth and Ordel send Greyhaven their first joint schedule. It gives the ferries fixed crossing hours and assigns the first open grain slots by the date each load was booked. Both assemblies still have to approve the purchase of a new dredger. The forewoman has signed the schedule herself and attached the three days of readings, including the one the clerks made her apprentice take twice.

Aurelia receives it the following morning. Her registrar has put the unsigned municipal form on top of the packet.

“Can we register a schedule under their names?” she asks.

“Yes. There’s a register for agreements whose parties have a disputed legal status. The names go in as supplied. We attach the decree separately.”

“Then send that back to them. Let them see the attachment before they sign.”

When the clerk reads the reply in Greyhaven, Sera asks for a copy of the schedule without the forms. She sends one to each ferry landing. By the time the Senate has finished debating what to call Nareth and Ordel in the Imperial register, the ferryman has chalked tomorrow’s crossing hours onto his board.
