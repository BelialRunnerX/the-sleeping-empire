## Played record

**The Right to Leave**
*Greyhaven harbour · Port office · Later that evening*

You allow the company to send a representative on two conditions: the harbourmaster’s staff must supervise the visit from arrival to departure, and nobody can be compelled to give a statement.

The harbourmaster reads the conditions back to the clerk. “Ask the passengers in private, before the representative arrives. If someone says no, the company doesn’t need to know who declined.”

The reply comes while a steward is collecting the empty stew bowls. The company accepts. Its representative, a woman named Tavin Marr, is already aboard a courier waiting outside the harbour. The harbourmaster assigns her a separate landing and a room in the port office. The tug’s crew still has no city entry visas.

By the time Marr arrives, rain has darkened her shoulders. An officer walks her in and takes a seat by the door. Her visitor badge reads **PORT OFFICE ONLY**.

Nessa declines. The man with the battered suitcase agrees to come, but tells the harbourmaster beforehand that he has a question, not a statement. He sets a square brass key on the table.

“This is for a locker at your station,” he says to Marr. “My sister is still there. When I left, she told me she wanted to come too. Can she walk out?”

Marr looks at the key. “I can find out.”

“That’s what I came to ask,” he says. Then he looks to the harbourmaster. “Am I done?”

“Yes,” she says, and the officer opens the door. Marr doesn’t try to stop him.

She does, however, ask to speak to you once he’s gone. “I can check whether his sister is on the station,” she says. “But that isn’t the same as finding out whether she can leave.”
