### Command Is the Art of Letting Go

📍 **Lumenfall Free Adventurers' Guild, Veyrath** · **9 Deepfall, 13,627 ICE · Afternoon** · Operational simulator, Station Three

Garrick gestures impatiently toward the third station. “If the two of you have completed your symposium on delegation—Clarissa Somnion.”

Clarissa steps into a holographic wilderness relief camp. Rain pounds the translucent roof of its command tent. Beyond it, a landslide has cut the northern evacuation road; eighty-six people queue beside three shuttle buses while a field hospital treats the injured.

**Scenario Forty-Four: Ashfall Refuge.** There are **312 civilians**, including **41 patients** requiring assisted transport; **24 responders**; four functional buses; two heavy rescue crawlers; and a rescue shuttle due in **72 minutes**. A toxic ash plume will make exposed operations unsafe in **65 minutes**. The southern road remains open, but its bridge is rated for light vehicles only.

Three people speak at once: the medical lead wants the first bus for patients; the logistics chief wants it for food and batteries; the security officer wants everyone moved immediately.

Clarissa's first order is quiet. “One voice at a time. Medical triage first. Then vehicle loads. Then we choose where each person goes.”

She assigns the medical lead complete authority over patient priority, has engineers certify axle loads, sends a scout crawler to check the southern road, and establishes a common dispatch board so the teams stop issuing incompatible orders. She does not personally fix a generator when the camp mechanic can do it.

At minute eighteen, the shuttle reports a thirty-minute delay. At minute twenty-five, an ash gust knocks out the main repeater. Clarissa has already ordered runners and local radio relays as backups; the camp does not lose command.

The crucial decision comes at minute thirty-one. The hospital's oxygen reserves are dwindling, and the buses cannot cross the light bridge at full load. Rather than overload it, she organizes staged transfers: patients and attendants move first in properly weighted vehicles, while the rescue crawlers shuttle the remaining civilians to a sheltered loading point behind a ridge. Engineers brace the approach road—not the bridge—and certify each subsequent load. The delayed shuttle takes the last critical patients from that protected site.

At minute sixty-two, the final responder enters cover. The simulated ash wall arrives three minutes later. All **312 civilians and 24 responders** are safe, all 41 assisted-transport patients accounted for, and no bridge or vehicle is lost.

Garrick reads the independent scoring log twice. “No unauthorized risk. Excellent delegation. Clear priorities. You preserved the team's ability to function when communications failed.”

**CLARISSA SOMNION — OPERATIONAL APTITUDE: 100/100.**
