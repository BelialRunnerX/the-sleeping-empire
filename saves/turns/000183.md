### The Limits of Measurement

📍 **Lumenfall Free Adventurers' Guild, Veyrath** · **9 Deepfall, 13,627 ICE · Afternoon** · Magical aptitude laboratory

You approach the measuring columns before any candidate steps onto the testing circle.

The chamber is surprisingly restrained: six translucent crystal pillars set into a black ceramic floor, with silver conductor rings surrounding a central dais. Behind thick observation glass, a technician in a pale blue coat adjusts an array of hand-sized reference stones. No spellfire is being released. The columns hum faintly, each with its own independent readout.

Garrick follows your inspection. “These are not weapons, Somion. They measure what a subject *chooses to emit*, not what can be forcibly extracted.”

The technical labels are straightforward:

- **Output:** controlled power delivered into a calibrated absorption lattice.
- **Precision:** stability and accuracy of a small prescribed effect.
- **Control:** stopping, reversing and modulating that effect on command.
- **Breadth:** distinct spell disciplines demonstrated under identical safety limits.
- **Endurance:** repeatability under a bounded, voluntary workload.

Your inspection reveals a practical distinction the guild's advertising fails to emphasize: **the containment system has a finite rating, and the display has a finite range**. The strongest column is intended to certify performance up through the guild's usual highest examination band; beyond that it records **ABOVE INSTRUMENT RANGE**, not a higher number. Its independent shutdown relay can interrupt the test lattice, but cannot guarantee suppression of an arbitrarily powerful external caster. No device here could meaningfully impose an upper bound on your own abilities—or Celeste's.

The safety arrangement is nevertheless sensible for ordinary applicants: a passive initial scan, voluntary low-output demonstrations, monitored absorption, physical isolation between test lanes and a hard abort before the equipment approaches saturation. You find no sign that Garrick intends to force a dangerous full-power discharge.

Celeste reads the upper range marking and tilts her head. “So the machine can tell you when it has stopped being useful.”

“Correct,” Garrick answers.

“How refreshingly self-aware.”

Clarissa studies the narrow precision lane. “Then our scores should measure performance against the assignment, not how dramatically we exceed the hardware.”

Garrick nods. “Exactly. A perfect score doesn't require blowing past the ceiling. For anyone above the range, we'll record the limitation honestly and assess safe command of the power actually demonstrated.”

He turns to you. “Does that answer your concerns, Elys Somion? We can begin with a low-energy calibration, or discuss alternative instrumentation first.”
