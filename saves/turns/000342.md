## Played record

**For the Record**
*League Senate chamber · Morning*

You ask the Senate to invite the envoy back. The chair sends the request in the League’s name and calls a short closed session so the harbour witnesses’ names stay off the public record.

The envoy arrives with rain beaded on their sleeves. As they take their seat, you lay out what Greyhaven knows and what it only suspects. The pilot says the company ordered him to follow a working passenger ship and report its berth. On the previous pickup, a deckhand logged an objection before the order to transfer those aboard arrived. A passenger says a guard took his suitcase and told him to follow. His sister’s recording describes being told she needed a supervisor’s signature to leave. She’s since reached her brother from outside the station.

“The concern,” the chair says, “is whether the company treats people it rescues as people it’s entitled to take back. We don’t know how widespread that is, or whether it’s still happening.”

You ask the envoy how the League can leave an official intergalactic statement for others to review without taking on the investigation itself.

“File a notice of concern,” the envoy says. They slide a blank form across the table. “The League signs it as the receiving jurisdiction. Name the company and describe what brought the matter to your attention. Mark which facts your harbourmaster verified and which came from testimony. The registry sends the notice to the tug’s flag authority and the authority that licenses the station. They decide whether to open a review.”

The envoy taps a smaller box at the bottom of the form. **WITNESS MATERIAL HELD LOCALLY.**

“Tick that if you want reviewers to know records exist without sending the statements or recordings. If someone asks for them, you’ll know who’s asking and why.”

The chair turns the form so you can read it. “I can have the clerk draw that up without putting a single passenger’s name on it.”
