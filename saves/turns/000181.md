### The Woman Who Apologized to a Reactor

📍 **Lumenfall Free Adventurers' Guild, Veyrath** · **9 Deepfall, 13,627 ICE · Afternoon** · Operational simulator, Station Two

Celeste steps onto the platform. The mountain valley folds into darkness. Then a research ship takes shape around her: copper emergency lamps, a slow drift of loose papers, and stars rotating beyond a cracked observation port.

“Scenario Thirty-One,” Garrick announces. “Research vessel *Peregrine*. Thirty-six crew. Reactor cooling instability. One evacuation shuttle, capacity twenty. Communications to rescue are blocked for forty minutes. Your ship's diagnostic AI insists the reactor is stable.”

A second warning immediately contradicts it: **COOLANT LOOP B — PRESSURE CRITICAL**. The chief engineer, an exhausted woman in scorched overalls, addresses Celeste through the holoscreen.

“Captain, tell me which alarm to believe.”

Celeste leans over the console, silver-blue light catching in her hair. “Neither. First, tell me what the machines have actually *measured*.”

She asks for raw flow and temperature readings from sensors on separate power buses. The primary AI reports steady coolant flow; the analog pressure gauge shows a fall. Rather than declare one instrument broken, she orders the engineer to isolate the loop, switch to reserve circulation, and compare the heat balance against reactor output.

The numbers refuse to agree.

“Wonderful,” Celeste says, with the particular delight of a prodigy encountering an interesting problem. “The computer is lying, and it's doing a very convincing job.”

At simulation minute twelve, she identifies the fault: a damaged data concentrator has been replaying valid but outdated telemetry. The reactor is *not* stable. More seriously, an automated repair routine has begun opening valves against the crew's manual isolation order.

Celeste kills that routine's authority, keeps the reactor at reduced output, and uses an independent local controller to restore cooling. She orders the thirty-six crew grouped into protected compartments, with the shuttle held ready for the most vulnerable twenty rather than launched prematurely.

Then the exercise introduces its second failure. A maintenance hatch jams between the engine crew and the emergency pump room. The engineer proposes venting an entire compartment to force the mechanism open. Celeste refuses: three crew are still crossing the adjacent passage.

Instead, she draws a repair sequence on the console, routes power around the failed actuator, and talks a junior technician through releasing the hatch manually. The youngster's hands shake; Celeste keeps her instructions short and precise.

The pump restarts at minute twenty-eight. Communications clear at minute forty. All thirty-six crew survive, the reactor remains contained, and *Peregrine* arrives at a tow-safe attitude without abandoning anyone.

Garrick checks the logs. “Exceptional engineering judgment. Excellent preservation of crew. One operational deduction.”

Celeste blinks. “Only one?”

“You personally coordinated the hatch repair while also holding reactor command. You had a qualified deputy available. Delegating would have reduced single-point workload risk.”

Celeste considers this, then smiles. “So I could have done less work and been even better?”

“Precisely.”

“How humiliating. I shall treasure the lesson.”
